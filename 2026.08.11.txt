



Some key strains at the current stage include PJ.2.1, RF.5.3.1, RF.5.4, PQ.16.1.1, RK.1, and SW.2.1, among others.


The PJ.2.1 strain accounts for a relatively high proportion in the Americas. It is a descendant of the MC.10.1.7 strain. Compared to the JN.1 strain, it differs by 21 spike protein mutations. Similar to the descendants of the TA.1 strain, it carries a large number of spike protein mutations.


Some key spike protein mutations in the PJ.2.1 strain include: The S:T547K mutation, which appears to be one of the defining mutations of the BA.1 strain and shows convergence in later strains such as the JN.1 and XFG families, although no clear benefit is currently observed; The S:N439K mutation, which showed convergence in early SARS-CoV-2 strains but now rarely occurs, though mutations like S:N417T have re-emerged; And the S:W452K mutation, which seems to be a very rare mutation, but some mutations among the convergent ones were also rare in the past.


S:V1264L mutation: This mutation has shown convergence in the XFG, NB.1.8.1, XFJ, and RF.5 families, making it one of the current hotspots for convergent mutations.

S:R346T mutation: This mutation appears in the BF.7 and BQ.1.1 strains, as well as in the XFG and LF.7 families; its convergent evolutionary characteristics have demonstrated growth advantages over multiple years.


S:H445P mutation: This mutation has undergone convergence multiple times in the LF.7 and NB.1.8.1 families.

S:S446T mutation: Although this mutation has not shown any threat in the current lineages, a similar mutation, S:S446N, has appeared in the XFG and PQ.16.1.1 families. Currently, some regions are still monitoring sub-lineages within the SV.2.1 strain that carry the S:S446N mutation.


S:S621P mutation: The S:S621P mutation is a reversion. In the BA.2.86 strain, this site was the S:P621S mutation. The effect of this mutation cannot be determined yet, but currently, carrying reversion mutations—such as the S:I478T mutation—has conferred significant growth advantages.
S:A623S mutation: This is a relatively key mutation, although it is uncertain what benefit it provides.
S:R190S mutation: The S:R190S mutation is found in both the XFG and LF.7 families, and it has played a key role in the spread of the strains on many occasions.
S:T22N mutation: Almost all currently circulating strains, with the exception of the BA.3.2 family, possess this mutation.
S:Q675K mutation: This mutation is also a relatively important one, though it did not have a particularly significant impact previously.


In India, the RF.5.3.1 and RF.5.4 strains are predominant, but in certain regions, another descendant of the RF.5.3 strain is also noteworthy. This strain carries the S:D420N and S:N417T mutations, which show convergence with the PQ.16.1.1 strain, as well as an S:T572I mutation, which also shows convergence with the NB.1.8.1 family. It remains to be seen whether a PQ.16.1.1 sub-lineage carrying the S:T572I mutation will stand out from the multitude of branches in the future.


In Mexico, the SW.2.1 strain of the XFG.6 family is spreading in the region. The advantage of the SW.2.1 strain is primarily attributed to the S:L441I, S:S446N, S:K529T, and S:N641K mutations. Among these, the S:S446N and S:N641K mutations are key convergent mutations within the XFG family, and these two convergent mutations have also been observed multiple times in the PQ.16.1.1 family.

In China, the PQ.16.1.1 strain continues to maintain its dominant position, with its primary sub-lineage, SV.2.1, being the main driver. However, SV.1.2 and other unnamed branches of SV.1 are also challenging SV.2.1. Currently, it is at least certain that SV.2.1, SV.2.2, SV.1.2, and SV.1.2.1 are all growing steadily.


Within the NB.1.8.1 family, a sub-lineage of the RK.1.2 strain carrying the S:S446N mutation is noteworthy. In the PQ.16.1.1 family, relatively convergent mutations include S:N439K, S:S446N, S:N641K, S:T572I, and S:V1264L; there are even branches possessing two or three mutations simultaneously, but none seem capable of challenging SV.2.1’s status at present. In fact, SV.2.1 itself has also developed some advantageous branches.
